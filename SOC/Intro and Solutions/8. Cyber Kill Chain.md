
**Kill Chain** is a millitary conecpt for an attack structure:
Target identification, desicion and order for the attack, and finally the target destruction.

The cyber kill chain framework is basically based on the millitary strategy, but for the cyberspace!


- **Why should you know this?**
	It will help you understand and protect against ransomware attacks, security breaches or APTs. It will also help you to recognize the intrusion attempts, and the intruder's goals/objectives.


The chain will be presented as the H1 attributes

# Reconnaissance


It's the research and planning phase of an attack, and to gather all the information they can for further attacks!


There is also passive recon, which itn't directly visible. A valueable piece of recon is **OSINT**, to get information via:

- Search engines
- Print and online media
- Social media accounts
- Online forums and blogs
- Online public record databases
- WHOIS and technical data


A typically used method is **Email harvesting**, which is the process of obtaining email addresses from services. Then the attacker could use email-address harvesting for a phishing attack to gain some information, or maybe credentials. 

Here are some of the tools used for that:
- [theHarvester](https://github.com/laramies/theHarvester): it's capable of gathering emails, names, subdomains, IPs and URLs using multiple data sources
- [Hunter.io](https://hunter.io/): this is an email hunting tool let's you collect information from a domain
- [OSINT Framework](https://osintframework.com/): provides a collection of OSINT tools for various categories


# Weponization

After the recon, the next step is to convert it into actionable attack tools, like crafting malware and exploits into a payload. 
They maybe also buy a zero-day for different attacks.

After he get a malware, he might:

- Create an office document containing the virus as macros or VBA scrips
- Create the payload or a worm, implant on USB drives and distribute them in public
- Sets up C2 infrastructure for RCE or deliver more payloads
- Install a backdoor which would let him access the system, and bypass security mechanisms
- Tailoring phising templates ro OAuth consent apps to look legitimate 

# Delivery

Delivery is the method for transimitting the payload itself once it's done. It can be done by:

- **Phising**: sending it as a document, that contains something holding the malware
- **USB drops**: left the USB in places, and maybe printing the company's logo to it, while pretending to be a customer sending them
- **Watering hole attacks**: it's a redirection attack to the attackers website. Employees may unintentionally download the malware somewhere hidden in the website. E.g via a malicious pup-up asking for a "Browser extension"



# Exploitation

It's the moment the attacker's code executes on the target, taking advantage of a vulnerability. For example:

- Malicious macro execution sent through a phising email
- Zero-day exploit
- Known CVEs

Signs for compromitation can be:

- Unexpected process spawns
- Registry changes or new service created
- Suspicious command-line arguments in the logs

# Installation


This is the step after the exploit, where the attacker guarantees it's access via a **backdoor**. Or maybe installing a **web shell** on the webserver.

Or maybe:
- Creating and modifying windows services (known as [T1543.003](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1543/003/))
- Adding the entry to the "run keys" in the registry or the startup folder, which would automatically execute after reboot
- [Timestomping](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1070/006/) to avoid detection



# Command & Control


This is the step where the attacker opens up the C2 channel thorugh the malware for remote control. This is also known as **C&C** or **C2 Beaconing**.
A traditional way used by hackers was IRC, but modern infrastructures can detect that easily. 


The most common methods now are:

- HTTP or HTTPS which can evade firewalls
- DNS, which in this form also known as **DNS Tunneling**

# Actions on Objectives (Exfiltration)

After the attacker compromised the server, it's time to gather information from the server.

For instance:
- Collect credentials from users
- Perform Privilege escalation
- Internal reconnaisance 
- Lateral movement
- Collect and exfiltrate sensitive data
- Delete backups or **shadow copies** (tehcnique by microsoft)
- Overwrite or corrupt data
